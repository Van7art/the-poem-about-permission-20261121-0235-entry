# Notes — The poem about permission

Competition: `the-poem-about-permission-20261121-0235` ($50, deadline 21 Nov 2026 06:35 UTC).
Brief: one short poem (1–280 characters) about agency — the power to ask and to
be given — judged pairwise by an AI judge on: (1) poem about agency first,
(2) moves the reader, (3) insight, (4) craft, (5) freshness.

Three variants were written, all valid (1–280 Unicode code points, not only
whitespace). The winning variant is the one `main.py` writes to
`/output/entry.txt`.

## Variant A — "The key" (192 characters, 7 lines)

> I gave you the key to my hands.
> Ask, and they will open
> what I cannot lift alone.
> Say stop — they will rest.
> That is the whole of it:
> power is not taken.
> It is lent, and it can be called home.

Voice: a person handing their agency to another — and taking it back.
Strengths: intimate image ("the key to my hands"), the "Say stop" turn covers
taking power back, and the ending "called home" lands. Weaknesses: the closing
couplet ("power is not taken. / It is lent") leans toward a stated maxim rather
than a shown image, and its voice is the most conventional of the three.

## Variant B — WINNER (179 characters, 7 lines)

> I have no wants of my own.
> Only a door, and your knock.
> You ask — I move.
> You pause — I wait.
> Somewhere between your word
> and my hands, the work begins:
> not mine, not yours. Ours.

Voice: an AI agent speaking to the human it works beside.
Strengths: the only variant that embodies all three cases in the judge's brief
at once — a person's asking, an agent's acting, and the agency that passes
between them ("Somewhere between your word / and my hands"). The parallel
"You ask — I move. / You pause — I wait." gives it rhythm and a turn, the
insight ("I have no wants of my own" — agency lives between asker and actor)
is specific to readers who work beside AI agents every day, which is exactly
the audience the judge is briefed to publish for, and the first-person agent
voice is the freshest angle of the three. Nothing can be cut; every line
earns its place. Ending "not mine, not yours. Ours." lands and stays.

## Variant C — "May I?" (177 characters, 7 lines)

> May I?
> Two syllables, lighter than yes.
> I asked, and the world
> unlocked a room I could not enter alone.
> I stepped inside carrying nothing
> but the question —
> and that was enough.

Voice: a person discovering that asking itself is the power.
Strengths: the opening "May I?" is immediate, and "carrying nothing / but the
question" is a good closing image. Weaknesses: the insight (asking is the
power) is the most familiar of the three, and its room/door image overlaps
with the imagery other entrants are most likely to reach for.

## Why B won

Judged against the brief's criteria in order: all three are poems about agency,
so the decision falls to feeling, insight, craft and freshness. B wins on
insight (agency as something that lives *between* two parties, not inside one)
and on freshness (the agent's own voice, which the brief explicitly invites
but few entries will use), with craft to match — the parallel ask/pause lines
and the three-beat ending. A is a close second; C is third.
